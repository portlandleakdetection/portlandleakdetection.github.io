Every job starts with locating, not digging. The methods stay non-invasive: acoustic listening picks up the sound of escaping water, thermal imaging maps temperature differences, tracer gas follows the path of a leak, and a sewer camera shows the inside of a line directly.

Slab leaks

Water lines under a concrete slab can leak for weeks before the surface shows anything. Listening equipment and pressure checks narrow the spot so only a small section needs opening. Read more: slab leak detection.

Underground and water line leaks

Buried supply lines fail quietly. A moving meter with everything off, soggy ground in dry weather, or a sudden bill increase all point the same way. Tracing the line first avoids trenching the whole yard: underground leak detection.

Sewer camera inspection

Slow drains and recurring backups deserve a look inside the pipe before anyone quotes a dig. A camera run shows roots, offsets and breaks on screen: sewer camera inspection.

Pool and spa leaks

Pools lose water to evaporation too, so the first job is measuring. Bucket tests and pressure checks separate normal loss from a plumbing or shell leak: pool leak detection.

Emergency, repair, thermal and commercial

Emergency service available for active leaks: emergency leak detection. Once located, repair options range from a spot fix to a reroute or full repipe: leak repair. Thermal imaging helps where pipes hide behind walls or under floors: thermal imaging. Larger buildings get the same locate-first approach at scale: commercial leak detection.
